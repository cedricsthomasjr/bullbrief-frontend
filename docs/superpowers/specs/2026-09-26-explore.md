# Explore sectors

- **Date:** 2026-09-26
- **Status:** In progress
- **Slug:** `explore`
- **Architecture docs:** `docs/architecture/explore.md`, `backend-sync/README.md`

A public Explore page gives a new visitor a way into a Brief without typing a ticker, then a map of the 11 sectors. A Brief shows nearby companies from the existing peers route.

This repo is the frontend. The live list is served by `app/api/explore/sectors`. The same rules are packed for the Flask backend in `backend-sync/`.

## Behavior

- Way-in: the six landing tickers, then three Compare presets. These are constants, not screener output.
- Map: Technology through Communication Services, in that fixed order. Six companies, largest `marketCap` first, titled "Largest by market cap".
- A second share class of the same company takes one slot. Symbols must be in `public/tickers.json`. ETFs and funds are dropped.
- A thin screener result, a failed call, or a missing `FMP_API_KEY` fills that sector entirely from the hand basket. Those rows are titled "Companies in this sector" and omit the market-cap figure.
- The assembled payload is cached for 12 hours.
- If the route fails, the way-in block still renders and the map says the lists could not be loaded.
- On a Brief, three peers sit under the company name. The Peers section lists up to six, with Open Brief and Add to Compare. `source: sector_static` uses the companies-in-this-sector label. A failed peer request leaves the line out. The Brief does not call the screener.

## Known limitation

A live list is the largest screener rows that are also in the ticker catalog, after dual-class collapse, cached up to 12 hours. It is not a complete index reconstitution. The Next.js cache lives in the server process. The Flask pack uses the same 12-hour window in process memory unless a later deploy adds Redis.
